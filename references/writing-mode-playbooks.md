# Writing Mode Playbooks

Choose a mode from user intent. Combine modes only when the premise naturally needs both.

## Forum Thread

Use for fast comedy, public misunderstanding, gossip, identity reveal, or group chaos.

Structure:

- Thread title with a strong situation, not the whole plot
- Original post: clear premise and one immediate question
- Replies: escalate through jokes, clues, wrong guesses, and one or two sharp observers
- Turn: evidence changes the crowd's interpretation
- Payoff: reveal, reversal, or soft emotional landing

Avoid:

- All commenters sounding like one person
- Replies that only explain canon
- Overusing internet slang until the character emotion disappears

## Chat Log

Use for lightweight intimacy, group dynamics, planning, teasing, or found-phone style snippets.

Structure:

- Participant list and context
- Short message bursts with timestamps only if useful
- Image/file placeholders only when they move the story
- Rhythm of interruption, typo, silence, and callback

Avoid:

- Long essay messages in chat form
- Unclear speaker identity
- Same sentence pattern for every participant

## Viewing Or Reaction

Use when characters watch canon/future/alternate scenes and the draw is emotional commentary.

Structure:

- Viewing rule and boundary
- First clip that feels safe or funny
- Second clip that destabilizes assumptions
- Private reaction leaking into public space
- After-scene choice, reconciliation, or new mission

Avoid:

- Retelling canon at length
- Characters only gasping without changing
- Spoilers that remove all tension too early

## Identity Reveal

Use for "掉马", hidden relationship, secret competence, secret account, or mistaken identity.

Structure:

- Establish the mask and why it matters
- Drop small clues
- Let a third party or public scene pressure the secret
- Reveal through action or evidence
- Show consequence, not just shock

Avoid:

- Reveal with no cost
- Everyone instantly accepting without character-specific reactions

## Daily Sweet Story

Use for comfort, domestic detail, healing, light romance, or low-conflict emotional satisfaction.

Structure:

- Small ordinary problem
- Character-specific care or teasing
- Sensory domestic anchor
- Micro-conflict or hesitation
- Quiet payoff

Avoid:

- Sugar without personality
- Generic pet names or physical templates
- Ending every paragraph with an abstract feeling

## Comedy Bit Or Segment

Use for short posts, meme-like premises, and easy sharing.

Structure:

- One absurd but clear premise
- Three beats of escalation
- One line that recontextualizes the setup
- Optional final softer note

Avoid:

- Explaining the joke
- Too many side characters

## Canon Drama

Use for plot-heavy, canon-adjacent, mission, case, battlefield, school, workplace, or historical setting.

Structure:

- Canon-consistent setting constraint
- External problem with stakes
- Character decision under pressure
- Emotional subtext through action
- Consequence that changes the next chapter

Avoid:

- Modern slang in incompatible settings
- Invented world facts when uncertainty can be left vague

## Serial Collection

Use for multi-post projects.

Structure:

- Series promise and stable motif
- Chapter 1: premise and hook
- Chapter 2: complication and relationship movement
- Chapter 3: consequence and stronger question
- Recurring tag set and collection name

Avoid:

- Switching CP, tone, or core conflict without warning
- Ending every chapter with the same style of cliffhanger
